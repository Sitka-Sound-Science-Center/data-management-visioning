# Sitka Sound Science Center – Research Data and Open Science Statement

At the **Sitka Sound Science Center (SSSC)**, we are committed to the principles of **Open Science** as a means to promote **transparency, reproducibility, equity, and collaboration** in scientific research. As a community-centered research institution in Southeast Alaska, we recognize our responsibility to steward data in ways that are not only scientifically rigorous but also **ethically grounded and culturally responsive**.

## Our Commitments

### 1. Reproducibility and Transparency
- We document our data collection, processing, and analysis protocols in detail, following field-specific best practices.
- Metadata standards are applied consistently to ensure that datasets are understandable and reusable by others.
- Whenever possible, we use open-source tools and software to enable reproducibility and minimize barriers to access.

### 2. Open Access to Data
- We make research data publicly available through trusted repositories (e.g., Arctic Data Center, Dryad, NOAA NCEI), in accordance with funder and institutional requirements.
- Sensitive data, including those involving Indigenous knowledge or community-specific ecological information, are protected under principles of **data sovereignty** and shared only with explicit consent.

### 3. Ethical Collaboration and Data Sovereignty
- We collaborate with local communities and Indigenous partners to co-design research and data-sharing protocols.
- We follow the **CARE Principles** for Indigenous Data Governance—*Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics*—alongside the **FAIR Principles** for data management.

### 4. Scientific Integrity
- We uphold rigorous standards for data quality, peer review, and documentation to support credible, unbiased science.
- We encourage researchers to preregister studies, publish null results, and embrace open peer review where possible.

### 5. Long-Term Studies
- We are committed to supporting and maintaining long-term environmental, ecological, and community-based monitoring projects.
- We ensure the continuity, accessibility, and stewardship of longitudinal datasets for future generations of researchers and residents.

### 6. Public Trust and Transparency
- We believe that transparent data practices are essential for building public trust in science.
- We provide open summaries of research findings and data use in accessible formats for community members, educators, and local decision-makers.

### 7. Continuous Improvement
- We regularly review and update our internal data management protocols based on feedback, new technologies, and evolving standards.
- We support training and capacity-building for researchers and community partners in open data practices.

### Quality Assurance / Quality Control (QA/QC)

We distinguish between and monitor three key data states:

- **Raw Data** – unprocessed, direct from instruments or observations  
- **Applied Data** – cleaned and formatted for analysis  
- **Synthetic Data** – derived or simulated for modeling or public release
